Question 1 of 40Dermatology High-yield concept
Itchy, purple, polygonal, flat-topped papules on the wrists with fine white lacy lines on their surface (Wickham's striae) are characteristic of:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: C. Lichen planus

Explanation

Question 3 of 40Biochemistry High-yield concept
An infant presents with fasting hypoglycaemia, massive hepatomegaly, lactic acidosis, hyperuricaemia and hyperlipidaemia. Which enzyme is deficient?
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: D. Glucose-6-phosphatase

Explanation

This is Von Gierke disease (GSD type I). Without glucose-6-phosphatase the liver cannot release free glucose from either glycogenolysis or gluconeogenesis, causing severe fasting hypoglycaemia with lactic acidosis, hyperuricaemia and hyperlipidaemia. Acid maltase deficiency = Pompe (type II, cardiomegaly); debranching enzyme = Cori (type III, milder, no lactic acidosis); muscle phosphorylase = McArdle (type V, exercise cramps, myoglobinuria).

One-liner to remember: Von Gierke = G6Pase (type I); Pompe = acid maltase (heart); Cori = debranching; McArdle = muscle phosphorylase.

Question 4 of 40Medicine High-yield concept
The most common cause of ENDOGENOUS Cushing syndrome is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: B. ACTH-secreting pituitary adenoma (Cushing disease)

Explanation

Overall the commonest cause of Cushing syndrome is exogenous (iatrogenic) glucocorticoid use. Among endogenous causes, about 70–75% are ACTH-dependent pituitary microadenomas (Cushing disease). Ectopic ACTH (e.g., small-cell lung carcinoma, carcinoids) is about 10–15% and often presents with hypokalaemia and pigmentation. Adrenal adenoma/carcinoma are ACTH-independent with suppressed ACTH. A high-dose dexamethasone test suppresses cortisol in pituitary but not ectopic disease.

One-liner to remember: Most common overall = exogenous steroids; most common endogenous = pituitary adenoma (Cushing disease).

Question 8 of 40Radiology High-yield concept
Which phase of the cell cycle is MOST sensitive to radiation?
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: C. G2–M phase

Explanation

Cells are most radiosensitive in the G2 and M (mitosis) phases and most radioresistant in the late S phase (DNA synthesis, active repair by homologous recombination). This is why fractionation works: redistribution (reassortment) moves surviving cells into sensitive phases. The 4 R's of radiobiology: Repair, Reoxygenation, Redistribution, Repopulation (+ Radiosensitivity). Per the Law of Bergonié and Tribondeau, rapidly dividing, undifferentiated cells are the most radiosensitive.

One-liner to remember: Most radiosensitive = G2–M; most radioresistant = late S; 4 R's of radiobiology.
Concept: Radiosensitivity of cell cycle

✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: C. 0.5 mg/L

Question 14 of 40Physiology High-yield concept
The maximum fraction of filtered sodium is reabsorbed in which part of the nephron?
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: A. Proximal convoluted tubule

Question 17 of 40PSM / Community Medicine High-yield concept
The ability of a screening test to correctly identify those who HAVE the disease is called:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: B. Sensitivity

Explanation

Sensitivity = TP/(TP+FN), the true-positive rate. A highly sensitive test has few false negatives, so it is preferred for screening and a negative result rules OUT disease (SnNout). Specificity = TN/(TN+FP), the ability to identify those WITHOUT disease; a positive highly specific test rules IN disease (SpPin). Predictive values depend on prevalence, while sensitivity and specificity do not.

One-liner to remember: Sensitivity = TP/(TP+FN) (SnNout); specificity = TN/(TN+FP) (SpPin); PPV rises with prevalence.
Concept: Screening test indices
Question 18 of 40Surgery High-yield concept
A 60 kg adult has 40% TBSA burns. Using the Parkland formula, how much Ringer lactate should be given in the FIRST 8 hours from the time of burn?
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: B. 4800 mL

Explanation

Parkland formula: 4 mL × body weight (kg) × %TBSA burn = 4 × 60 × 40 = 9600 mL of Ringer lactate in the first 24 hours. HALF of it (4800 mL) is given in the first 8 hours counted from the TIME OF BURN (not from admission), and the rest over the next 16 hours. 9600 mL is the 24-hour total. The EARLIEST ECG change seen in hyperkalaemia is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: D. Tall, peaked (tented) T waves

Explanation

As serum K⁺ rises, the sequence is: tall peaked T waves first, then PR prolongation, P-wave flattening/loss, QRS widening, a sine-wave pattern, and finally VF/asystole. Question 21 of 40Pharmacology High-yield concept
The specific antidote used in paracetamol (acetaminophen) poisoning is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: B. N-acetylcysteine

Explanation

Toxic doses saturate glucuronidation/sulfation, so more paracetamol goes via CYP2E1 to the toxic metabolite NAPQI, which depletes glutathione and causes centrilobular (zone 3) hepatic necrosis. N-acetylcysteine replenishes glutathione and is most effective within 8–10 hours (the Rumack–Matthew nomogram guides treatment). Flumazenil is for benzodiazepines, fomepizole for methanol/ethylene glycol, and naloxone for opioids.

One-liner to remember: Paracetamol → NAPQI → zone 3 necrosis; antidote NAC (best within 8–10 h).
Concept: Paracetamol poisoning antidote

Explanation

A boy with self-mutilation (lip and finger biting), choreoathetosis, intellectual disability and hyperuricaemia most likely has a deficiency of:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: A. HGPRT

Explanation

Lesch–Nyhan syndrome is X-linked recessive complete deficiency of hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase (HGPRT), a purine SALVAGE enzyme. Question 39 of 40OBG High-yield concept
A woman with eclampsia is on magnesium sulphate. The EARLIEST sign of magnesium toxicity is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: D. Loss of patellar (deep tendon) reflex

Explanation

MgSO₄ is the drug of choice for prevention and treatment of eclamptic seizures. Toxicity is serum-level dependent: loss of the patellar reflex (~10 mEq/L) comes first, then respiratory depression (~12–15 mEq/L), then cardiac arrest (> 25 mEq/L). Before each dose, check patellar reflex present, RR above 12–16/min (per textbook) and urine output ≥ 30 mL/h (oliguria is a reason to withhold, since Mg is excreted renally, not a toxicity sign). Antidote: 10 mL of 10% calcium gluconate IV.

One-liner to remember: MgSO₄ toxicity: first sign = loss of knee jerk; antidote = 10 mL 10% calcium gluconate IV slowly.
Concept: Magnesium sulphate toxicity
