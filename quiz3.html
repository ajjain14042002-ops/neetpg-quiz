Question 1 of 40Microbiology Image-based
IMAGE-BASED: A man with HIV (CD4 count 40/µL) has headache and fever. This CSF preparation is shown. Which statement is CORRECT?
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: A. A negative India-ink preparation does not exclude the diagnosis; cryptococcal antigen (CrAg) is substantially more sensitive

Explanation

The image is an India-ink (negative) stain of CSF: round yeasts with a wide clear capsular halo, typical of Cryptococcus. Correct: India ink is rapid but only about 70–80% sensitive (lower in HIV-negative patients), so a negative result does not exclude disease. CrAg (lateral-flow or latex) on CSF or serum is >95–99% sensitive and is the test of choice; serum CrAg is also used to screen patients with CD4 <100. Why the others are wrong: India ink is not >99% sensitive. Candida does not produce this wide capsule and is identified by a germ-tube test, not India ink. Mucicarmine stains the capsule in tissue sections, not as a rapid CSF bedside test. Induction treatment is amphotericin B plus flucytosine, then fluconazole; always measure opening pressure.

One-liner to remember: Crypto: India ink shows the capsule but misses cases; CrAg is the most sensitive rapid test.
Concept: Cryptococcal meningitis – diagnosis

Question 2 of 40PSM / Community Medicine High-yield concept
The incidence of stroke is 20 per 1000 among hypertensives and 5 per 1000 among normotensives. Which statement is CORRECT?
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: B. Attributable risk percent is 75%, so three-quarters of the strokes among hypertensives are attributable to hypertension

Explanation

AR% = (20 − 5)/20 × 100 = 75%. Relative risk = 20/5 = 4 (not 75% and not 'AR% = 4'). The absolute attributable risk (risk difference) is 15 per 1000, not 15%. Why the others are wrong: they swap the names and the units of RR, AR and AR%. Always write the formula down before picking a number that appears in the stem.

One-liner to remember: Name the measure first: RR has no units (here 4); AR is a rate (15/1000); AR% is a percentage (75%).
Concept: Attributable risk (percent)

Question 3 of 40Pathology High-yield concept
Which of the following is NOT a feature of reversible cell injury?
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: A. Nuclear pyknosis, karyorrhexis and karyolysis

Explanation

Robbins: hallmarks of IRREVERSIBILITY are the inability to restore mitochondrial function and severe membrane damage. Nuclear pyknosis, karyorrhexis and karyolysis are light-microscopic features of necrosis (irreversible injury); on EM, large flocculent amorphous mitochondrial densities and plasma-membrane rupture mean the same thing. Why the others ARE reversible: hydropic swelling (the earliest change), membrane blebbing, ER dilatation, ribosomal detachment and myelin figures can all still recover if the insult is withdrawn. This stem is an EXCEPT/NOT question — swelling and blebbing are classic traps if you invert it.

One-liner to remember: Reversible = swelling, blebbing, myelin figures. Irreversible = mitochondrial flocculent densities, membrane rupture, nuclear pyknosis/karyorrhexis/karyolysis.
Concept: Reversible vs irreversible cell injury (EM)

Question 4 of 40Psychiatry High-yield concept
A 28-year-old with bipolar disorder has had four distinct manic or depressive episodes in the last 11 months. TSH is normal. The mood stabiliser of choice is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: D. Sodium valproate

Explanation

Four or more mood episodes in 12 months = rapid cycling. Correct: valproate (and other anticonvulsant mood stabilisers such as lamotrigine or carbamazepine) is preferred; lithium responds poorly in rapid cycling, although it remains first-line for classic euphoric mania and for suicide prevention. Why the others are wrong: lithium is the usual trap because it is the 'classic' mood stabiliser. Antidepressants (venlafaxine, amitriptyline and other TCAs/SNRIs) can induce switching and rapid cycling and should be avoided or tapered. Always check thyroid function, because hypothyroidism itself is associated with rapid cycling.

One-liner to remember: Rapid cycling (≥4 episodes/year): valproate > lithium; stop antidepressants; check TSH.
Concept: Rapid-cycling bipolar disorder – drug choice

Question 5 of 40Pediatrics High-yield concept
A 2-year-old has severe dehydration from acute diarrhoea and IV access is obtained. As per WHO Plan C, Ringer lactate should be given as:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: C. 30 mL/kg in 30 minutes, then 70 mL/kg in the next 2½ hours

Explanation

WHO Plan C uses 100 mL/kg of Ringer lactate (or normal saline) in two parts; the SPEED depends on age. A 2-year-old is ≥12 months, so 30 mL/kg is given in 30 minutes and 70 mL/kg in the next 2½ hours (total 3 hours). Why the others are wrong: 30 mL/kg in 1 hour then 70 mL/kg in 5 hours (total 6 hours) is the schedule for INFANTS under 12 months — the usual trap in the opposite direction from Quiz 2. 75 mL/kg ORS over 4 hours is Plan B (some dehydration). Repeated 20 mL/kg boluses are a shock-resuscitation approach, not the Plan C rehydration infusion.

One-liner to remember: Plan C: ≥12 months = 30 min + 2½ h; <12 months = 1 h + 5 h (30 then 70 mL/kg).
Concept: WHO Plan C – severe dehydration rate by age

Question 6 of 40Medicine High-yield concept
On day 3 of severe pneumonia, thyroid tests show low T3, high reverse T3 and a normal TSH. A week later, as the patient recovers, TSH is mildly raised and T4 is low-normal. The MOST likely explanation is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: D. Recovery from sick euthyroid syndrome (a transient TSH rise is expected; do not label this as primary hypothyroidism yet)

Explanation

Correct: during RECOVERY from non-thyroidal illness, TSH often rises transiently (sometimes above the reference range) as the axis resets; T4 may still be low-normal. Repeat thyroid tests after full recovery before diagnosing primary hypothyroidism. Why the others are wrong: primary hypothyroidism is suggested by a persistently high TSH with low T4 once the patient is well, and rT3 is not high in true hypothyroidism. A TSH-oma causes high TSH with high (not low-normal) free T4 and thyrotoxic clinical features. Factitious thyrotoxicosis suppresses TSH and raises T4/T3.

One-liner to remember: Sick euthyroid recovery can raise TSH transiently — repeat tests after the illness before starting thyroxine.
Concept: Sick euthyroid (non-thyroidal illness) syndrome

Question 7 of 40Psychiatry High-yield concept
A woman with bipolar disorder has had six mood episodes this year while taking lithium plus a recently added SNRI. TSH is normal. Besides reviewing adherence and substances, the MOST appropriate medication change is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: C. Taper and stop the antidepressant, and switch the mood stabiliser to valproate (or add an anticonvulsant mood stabiliser)

This is rapid cycling, likely worsened by the antidepressant. Correct: stop the SNRI (antidepressants induce switching) and use valproate (or another anticonvulsant mood stabiliser), which outperforms lithium in rapid cycling. Question 8 of 40Orthopedics Image-based
IMAGE-BASED: A child fell on an outstretched hand. The elbow and forearm X-rays are shown. The nerve MOST likely to be injured, and the expected deficit, is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: C. Posterior interosseous nerve — finger and thumb drop without wrist drop and without sensory loss

Explanation

The films show a fracture of the proximal ulna with dislocation of the radial head — a Monteggia fracture-dislocation (Bado type I when the head is anterior, the commonest type). The posterior interosseous nerve (deep branch of the radial nerve) winds around the radial neck through supinator and is stretched by the dislocated head. The result is finger and thumb drop (loss of EDC, EI, EPL) without wrist drop (ECRL is supplied more proximally by the radial nerve) and without cutaneous sensory loss. Why the others are wrong: AIN palsy is the classic nerve injury of a supracondylar humerus fracture, not Monteggia. The ulnar nerve is at risk in medial epicondyle fractures. Isolated superficial radial sensory loss is not the Monteggia pattern. Compare Galeazzi: distal-radius fracture + DRUJ dislocation.

One-liner to remember: Monteggia = ulna fracture + radial-head dislocation → PIN (finger drop, no wrist drop, no sensory loss).
Concept: Monteggia fracture-dislocation – nerve injury

Question 9 of 40Medicine High-yield concept
A young adult with known WPW presents with an irregular, very rapid, wide-complex tachycardia and a blood pressure of 70/40 mmHg with confusion. The MOST appropriate immediate treatment is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: D. Immediate synchronised DC cardioversion

Explanation

This is haemodynamically unstable pre-excited AF. Correct: immediate synchronised DC cardioversion is the treatment of choice in any unstable tachyarrhythmia, including pre-excited AF, and is not contraindicated in WPW. Why the others are wrong: verapamil, adenosine and digoxin are AV-nodal blockers and are contraindicated — they can accelerate conduction over the accessory pathway and cause VF. Question 10 of 40Surgery High-yield concept
A 52-year-old is recovering from SEVERE acute gallstone pancreatitis. CT shows large peripancreatic fluid collections. He is now afebrile and tolerating oral intake. The recommended timing of cholecystectomy is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: A. Delayed until the collections have substantially resolved, typically after about 6 weeks

Explanation

Correct: after SEVERE biliary pancreatitis with peripancreatic collections or necrosis, cholecystectomy is delayed (usually around 6 weeks) until the collections have substantially resolved, so that surgery is safer. Why the others are wrong: same-admission (or within about 2 weeks) cholecystectomy is the recommendation for MILD gallstone pancreatitis, which is the usual trap. Emergency cholecystectomy in the acute phase is not advised. Waiting for a second attack is unsafe — recurrent biliary events are common if the gallbladder is left in place indefinitely. Urgent ERCP is reserved for co-existing cholangitis or persistent biliary obstruction, not for routine 'clearance' in every severe case.

One-liner to remember: Severe biliary pancreatitis with collections → delay cholecystectomy ~6 weeks; mild disease → same admission.
Concept: Gallstone pancreatitis – timing of cholecystectomy

Question 11 of 40Pediatrics Image-based
IMAGE-BASED: A 10-month-old with this chest X-ray becomes deeply cyanosed and limp after a long cry; the systolic murmur is much softer than usual. Which drug is CONTRAINDICATED in this situation?
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: B. Intravenous dobutamine

Explanation

The X-ray shows a boot-shaped (coeur-en-sabot) heart with a narrow vascular pedicle, typical of tetralogy of Fallot. This is a hypercyanotic (Tet) spell: infundibular spasm increases right-to-left shunting, so less blood crosses the pulmonary valve and the murmur SOFTENS. Correct: inotropes such as dobutamine, isoprenaline and digoxin increase contractility, worsen infundibular spasm and deepen cyanosis — they are contraindicated. Why the others are useful: morphine reduces hyperpnoea and catecholamine drive; phenylephrine raises systemic vascular resistance and pushes blood into the lungs; propranolol relaxes the infundibulum and slows the heart. Also used: knee-chest position, oxygen and bicarbonate for acidosis.

One-liner to remember: Tet spell: knee-chest, O₂, morphine, fluids, propranolol, phenylephrine — NO inotropes (dobutamine, isoprenaline, digoxin).
Concept: Tetralogy of Fallot – hypercyanotic (Tet) spell

Question 12 of 40Surgery High-yield concept

✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: C. Urgent ERCP with sphincterotomy, then laparoscopic cholecystectomy during the same admission once the pancreatitis has settled

Explanation

Correct: co-existing cholangitis (or persistent biliary obstruction) is the indication for urgent ERCP (typically within 24–72 hours) with sphincterotomy. Because the pancreatitis itself is MILD, cholecystectomy should then be performed during the SAME admission once the pancreatitis has settled, to prevent recurrence. Why the others are wrong: emergency cholecystectomy in the acute phase, especially without treating cholangitis first, is not recommended. Cryptococcal meningitis is confirmed in a patient with advanced HIV. The preferred INDUCTION therapy is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: B. Amphotericin B plus flucytosine, followed by fluconazole consolidation

Explanation

Correct: standard induction is amphotericin B (or a lipid formulation) plus flucytosine for about 2 weeks, then fluconazole consolidation and prolonged maintenance, especially until immune reconstitution. Opening pressure must be measured and high pressure treated with repeated lumbar drainage. Why the others are wrong: low-dose fluconazole alone is inadequate induction (higher-dose fluconazole is only a fallback when amphotericin is unavailable). Question 16 of 40OBG High-yield concept
Which statement about the Government of India (DIPSI) guideline for GDM is CORRECT?
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: A. 75 g oral glucose is given irrespective of the last meal, and a 2-hour venous plasma glucose ≥140 mg/dL diagnoses GDM

Explanation

Correct: DIPSI uses 75 g glucose without regard to fasting, and diagnoses GDM at a 2-hour venous plasma glucose ≥140 mg/dL. Testing is recommended at the first ANC visit and again at 24–28 weeks if initially negative. Question 17 of 40Medicine High-yield concept
A septic ICU patient with no prior thyroid disease has low T3, low-normal T4, normal TSH and a raised reverse T3. The MOST appropriate action is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: C. Do not start thyroid hormone; treat the underlying illness (this is sick euthyroid / non-thyroidal illness)

Explanation

In non-thyroidal illness, cytokines and fasting reduce peripheral 5′-deiodinase, so less T4 is converted to T3 and more to reverse T3. Correct: low T3 with raised rT3 (TSH usually normal) is sick euthyroid syndrome; thyroid hormone is not given. A falling T4 marks more severe illness and a poorer prognosis. Why the others are wrong: low T3/T4 here does not mean primary hypothyroidism (that pattern is high TSH, low T4, and rT3 is not raised). Treating with T4 or T3 does not improve outcome and may be harmful. The raised rT3 is the laboratory clue that this is not true hypothyroidism.

One-liner to remember: Sick euthyroid: ↓T3, ↑rT3, TSH usually normal — do not treat with thyroxine. ↓T4 = severe illness.
Concept: Sick euthyroid (non-thyroidal illness) syndrome

A 14-month-old with clinically confirmed measles has no eye signs of vitamin A deficiency. As per WHO, vitamin A should be given as:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: B. 200,000 IU orally today, repeated the next day

Explanation

WHO measles treatment doses: <6 months 50,000 IU; 6–11 months 100,000 IU; ≥12 months 200,000 IU — given on day 1 and day 2 (a third dose later if there are ocular signs). Question 26 of 40Medicine High-yield concept
On day 8 of unfractionated heparin, platelets fall from 280 to 90 × 10⁹/L and a new pulmonary embolus is diagnosed. HIT is confirmed. Which of the following is CONTRAINDICATED at this moment?
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: A. Starting warfarin today as the sole anticoagulant, while the platelet count is still 90 × 10⁹/L

Explanation

This is type II HIT (typically day 5–10): thrombocytopenia plus paradoxical thrombosis. Correct: early warfarin, especially as monotherapy while platelets are still low, rapidly lowers protein C and can cause venous limb gangrene or warfarin-induced skin necrosis — it is contraindicated until platelets have recovered (usually ≥150 × 10⁹/L), and it must then be overlapped with a non-heparin agent. One-liner to remember: HIT: stop all heparin, start argatroban/fondaparinux; no LMWH, no early warfarin, no platelets unless bleeding.
Concept: Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia – management

Question 29 of 40Physiology High-yield concept
Which of the following hormones acts through a receptor that signals via the JAK–STAT pathway?
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: D. Erythropoietin

Explanation

Correct: erythropoietin uses a cytokine-type receptor with no intrinsic kinase; it recruits JAK2, which phosphorylates STAT proteins. Growth hormone, prolactin, leptin, G-CSF and many interleukins work the same way. Why the others are wrong: the insulin receptor (like IGF-1, EGF and PDGF receptors) is a receptor tyrosine kinase with its OWN intrinsic kinase — this is the usual mix-up, because both pathways involve tyrosine phosphorylation. ✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: D. Intravenous digoxin

Explanation

✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: D. 9 doses totalling 17 lakh IU

Explanation

Count the 2-lakh doses first: from 16–18 months to 5 years inclusive, every 6 months, is 8 doses (about 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 54 and 60 months). Add the 9-month 1-lakh dose: 9 doses. Question 32 of 40Psychiatry High-yield concept
A patient stable on lithium needs an additional drug. Which of the following is LEAST likely to raise the serum lithium concentration?
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: B. Theophylline

Explanation

Correct: theophylline (and acetazolamide and mannitol) increase lithium clearance and tend to lower, not raise, the level. Why the others raise lithium: hydrochlorothiazide causes mild volume contraction so the proximal tubule reabsorbs more sodium and lithium (levels may rise 25–40%). Under India's open vial policy, which opened vaccine must be discarded at the end of the immunisation session (or within 4 hours of reconstitution) and cannot be kept for 28 days?
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: A. Measles–rubella (MR) vaccine

Explanation

Correct: MR (like measles, BCG and JE) is a freeze-dried vaccine that is reconstituted before use. The open-vial policy does NOT cover reconstituted (lyophilised) vaccines; they are discarded 4 hours after reconstitution or at the end of the session, whichever is earlier. Question 35 of 40Medicine High-yield concept
A 19-year-old has recurrent cramps, BP 110/70 mmHg, K⁺ 2.6 mEq/L, metabolic alkalosis, urine chloride 50 mEq/L, low serum magnesium and low urinary calcium. He is not on diuretics. The MOST likely diagnosis is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: A. Gitelman syndrome

Explanation

Split metabolic alkalosis by urine chloride, then by blood pressure. Urine Cl⁻ >20 mEq/L is saline-resistant. Normal BP then points to Bartter, Gitelman or current diuretics — not mineralocorticoid excess. Correct: Gitelman (a defect like chronic thiazide use) presents in later childhood or adults with hypokalaemic alkalosis, high urine Cl⁻, hypomagnesaemia and hypocalciuria, and normal blood pressure. Why the others are wrong: Conn syndrome causes saline-resistant alkalosis WITH hypertension. Vomiting is saline-responsive (urine Cl⁻ typically <20). Bartter (a defect like loop-diuretic use) usually presents earlier in childhood, has normal or high urinary calcium and less consistently severe hypomagnesaemia, and may cause nephrocalcinosis.

One-liner to remember: Urine Cl >20 + normal BP + ↓Mg + hypocalciuria = Gitelman. Same labs + high BP = Conn/Liddle.
Concept: Metabolic alkalosis – chloride-responsive vs resistant

Explanation

Location plus skeletal maturity is the key. Correct: giant cell tumour arises in the epiphysis (often extending to the metaphysis and subchondral plate) AFTER physeal closure, typically at age 20–40. Question 37 of 40Medicine High-yield concept
A patient with prolonged vomiting has metabolic alkalosis, K⁺ 3.0 mEq/L and a urine chloride of 8 mEq/L. This alkalosis is BEST described as:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: C. Chloride-responsive (saline-responsive); the low urine chloride reflects extra-renal chloride loss

Explanation

Correct: urine Cl⁻ <15–20 mEq/L defines chloride- (saline-) responsive metabolic alkalosis — vomiting, nasogastric losses, post-hypercapnia, or past (not current) diuretic use. ✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: B. Gestational diabetes mellitus (non-fasting 75 g test; 2-hour value ≥140 mg/dL)

Explanation

Correct: DIPSI is a single-step test — 75 g oral glucose is given irrespective of the last meal, and a 2-hour venous plasma glucose ≥140 mg/dL diagnoses GDM. Question 40 of 40PSM / Community Medicine High-yield concept
In a cohort study, the incidence of coronary events is 36 per 1000 among smokers and 12 per 1000 among non-smokers. The attributable risk percent (AR%) is:
✓ Correct!
✗ Incorrect. Correct answer: C. 66.7%

Explanation

AR% = (incidence in exposed − incidence in unexposed) ÷ incidence in exposed × 100 = (36 − 12) ÷ 36 × 100 = 24/36 × 100 = 66.7%. This is the fraction of disease among the exposed that is attributable to the exposure. Why the others are wrong: 3 is the RELATIVE RISK (36 ÷ 12), which measures strength of association, not the attributable fraction. 24% confuses the absolute risk difference (24 per 1000) with a percentage. 33.3% is the unexposed incidence as a fraction of the exposed incidence (12 ÷ 36), which is 1 − AR% and is not the attributable risk.

One-liner to remember: AR% = (Ie − Iu)/Ie × 100; RR = Ie/Iu; risk difference = Ie − Iu (a rate, not a %).
Concept: Attributable risk (percent)
